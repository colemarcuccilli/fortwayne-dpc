You may have heard a doctor or a clinic say they offer "whole-person care." The phrase is common, and it can sound like a slogan. It is also a real term with a real meaning in primary care. This article explains what it means, where it comes from, and what it can look like when you visit your own doctor.

## Care that is not limited to one area

Many parts of medicine are organized around one organ system or one diagnosis. A specialist who treats the heart, for example, focuses on the heart. That focus is useful when you need it. Primary care works differently.

The American Academy of Family Physicians (AAFP) defines primary care physicians as providing "comprehensive, whole-person care with any undiagnosed ('undifferentiated') sign, symptom or health concern." The AAFP says this care is not limited by problem origin, organ system or diagnosis.

In plain words, a primary care doctor is the person you can bring a health concern to even when you do not know what it is or which kind of doctor it belongs to. The doctor looks at the concern in the context of the whole person, not just one part of the body.

:::note Worth knowing
The AAFP says primary care physicians provide "comprehensive, whole-person care" and that this care is not limited by problem origin, organ system or diagnosis.
:::

## The person, not only the problem

The AAFP definition also uses the words "person-centered." In primary care settings, it describes care as person- and family-oriented. That means the doctor considers who you are, and often who is in your family, and not only the symptom you walked in with.

The AAFP also describes the physician's role as addressing a large majority of personal health care needs and developing a sustained partnership with patients. A partnership implies two people working on something together, which is a different picture from a single visit where a problem is fixed and the relationship ends.

## Why time and continuity matter

Whole-person care depends on a relationship that lasts. The AAFP says the primary care physician assumes ongoing responsibility for the patient's care and maintains continuity through sustained relationships over time.

MedlinePlus, from the National Library of Medicine, describes the same idea from the patient's side. It says a primary care provider lets you build a trusting relationship with one medical professional over time and keeps your personal health care continuous. It adds that you and your primary care physician work as a team toward preventive care and the best measures for achieving and maintaining your health.

MedlinePlus presents this lasting relationship as part of how you and your doctor work toward preventive care and maintaining your health.

## Coordination across the health system

Looking at the whole person also means keeping track of the other care a person receives. The AAFP says primary care physicians ensure coordination of care across the health system, including specialists, hospitals and community services. The physician also advocates for the patient in coordinating that system.

If you see a specialist, go to a hospital or use a community service, your primary care doctor is meant to be the one who helps connect those pieces. That job is easy to overlook until you need it.

## What whole-person care can look like in practice

The term describes an approach, so it shows up in small ways. These are general things you can notice in any primary care relationship:

1. The doctor asks about more than the symptom you came in with.
2. You see the same doctor over time, so your history does not need to be explained again each visit.
3. Your family and your daily life are treated as part of the picture.
4. If you need a specialist or hospital care, someone helps tie it together.
5. You can bring up a concern that you are not sure how to describe.

None of this is a promise about any particular doctor. It is a way to read what you experience and decide whether your care fits the description.

## Questions to ask a doctor

If you are choosing a primary care doctor, or thinking about whether your current one meets your needs, these questions may help.

How long are visits, and will I see the same doctor each time? How do I reach the office when something comes up between visits? How does the doctor work with specialists and hospitals when I need them? Does the doctor talk with me about my own goals, or only about the problem in front of us?

Answers to these questions can tell you more than a phrase on a website. They show how the practice runs day to day.

## The short version

Whole-person care means a primary care doctor who is not limited by organ system or diagnosis, who knows you over time, who treats you as a person within a family, and who helps coordinate the rest of your care. Those ideas come from the AAFP definition of primary care and match how MedlinePlus describes the value of a lasting relationship with one provider. Use them as a guide when you look at your own care.

This article is general information and not medical advice. Talk with your doctor about your own care.
