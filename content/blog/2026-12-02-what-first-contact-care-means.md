When a health concern comes up, the first person you contact shapes what happens next. The phrase first contact care describes that role. It is one of the core ideas in the definition of primary care used by the American Academy of Family Physicians (AAFP), and understanding it helps you see what primary care is meant to do.

## What the phrase means

The AAFP says that a primary care physician serves as the patient's first point of contact for undifferentiated health concerns. Undifferentiated means the concern has no diagnosis yet. You have a symptom, a worry or a question, and you do not know what category it belongs to.

First contact care is the care you reach first for that kind of concern. It is where you start, before you know whether you need a specialist, a test, a procedure or none of those.

:::note Worth knowing
The AAFP says primary care physicians serve as the patient's first point of contact for undifferentiated health concerns.
:::

## Why the starting point matters

Without a first point of contact, a person has to guess where to go. Someone with a new symptom might not know whether the problem belongs to a heart doctor, a skin doctor or a stomach doctor. The first contact role takes that guess out of the process. A clinician who looks at the whole person receives the concern and decides what it needs.

This is also why the AAFP describes primary care as comprehensive, whole person care. It addresses a large majority of personal health care needs, whether a problem is biological, behavioral or social. First contact is the doorway into that range of care.

## First contact works together with other parts of primary care

The AAFP definition does not stop at first contact. It lists several features that work together.

Continuity is one. The AAFP describes continuity through sustained relationships over time. The same physician keeps ongoing responsibility for the patient across care settings and life stages. A first visit that begins a long relationship is different from a one time visit with a stranger.

Coordination is another. The AAFP says primary care physicians ensure coordination of care across the health system, including specialists, hospitals and community services. When a concern does need a specialist, the first contact clinician is the one who connects the pieces.

Comprehensiveness is the third. Primary care covers a wide range of needs, so most concerns can be handled in one place.

:::note Worth knowing
The AAFP definition describes primary care as including continuity through sustained relationships over time and coordination of care across the health system.
:::

## Who provides first contact care

According to the AAFP, primary care is delivered by physicians and their care teams. The primary care specialties it names are family medicine, general internal medicine, general pediatrics, geriatrics, and combined internal medicine and pediatrics.

Care teams can include nurse practitioners, physician assistants, social workers, pharmacists and others, usually led by a primary care physician. The AAFP says nurse practitioners, physician assistants and other clinicians may provide primary care services, but they should work as part of a collaborative team.

The AAFP also says physicians without primary care training may offer specific services, but not comprehensive, first contact, continuous care. And it states that primary care and family medicine are not interchangeable terms.

## A simple example of the idea

Think of someone who notices a change they cannot explain and has no idea who to call. Under the first contact idea, that person contacts primary care. The clinician listens, asks questions and then decides whether the concern can be handled there or needs another service. The person does not have to sort it out alone before making the first call, and that is the practical value of the definition.

## What first contact does not mean

First contact does not mean the first clinician handles everything personally. It means this clinician is the first point of contact and stays involved. Where a concern needs more than primary care can offer, the coordination feature applies. The first contact clinician helps link you to the right next step and keeps the overall picture.

It also does not mean you wait until something is seriously wrong. The AAFP's wording covers undifferentiated concerns, which can be anything from a new symptom to a general question about your health.

## Questions to ask about any primary care practice

Since first contact is a defining feature, it is fair to ask how a practice delivers it. A few questions are worth asking before you choose:

- How do I reach the practice with a new concern, and who responds?
- Will I see the same physician over time?
- How does the practice coordinate with specialists and other services?
- What does a visit cost, and what is covered by a membership or fee?

The answers tell you whether the practice offers the access and continuity the AAFP definition describes.
